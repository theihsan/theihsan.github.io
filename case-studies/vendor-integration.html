Third-Party AI & Attendance Platform — Structured Verification

Ultraction General Contracting LLC · Abu Dhabi, UAE · Vendor Accountability & Testing
01 — Context & Problem
A vendor's claims needed independent verification

The organization onboarded a third-party platform combining AI-assisted email handling with an attendance-tracking integration. Rather than accepting the vendor's feature claims at face value once delivered, a structured verification process was needed to confirm that what was promised actually matched what was delivered — and to hold the vendor to fixing what didn't.

02 — Environment
Two integrated modules, one Microsoft 365 environment

The platform integrated with the organization's Microsoft 365 environment across two modules: an AI-assisted email-handling layer, and an attendance-tracking integration. Both modules were new to the organization and had no prior internal usage history to benchmark against.

03 — My Responsibility
Contract risk review, formal testing, and accountability tracking

Ahead of rollout, I reviewed the vendor's professional services agreement for risk and flagged concerns around intellectual-property ownership terms and an incomplete deliverables schedule. Once the platform was delivered, I authored formal, itemized testing remarks against each promised capability, tracked the vendor's response to every item, and independently re-verified each claimed fix across multiple rounds rather than accepting the vendor's own changelog.

04 — Investigation
Testing against the promised feature set, item by item

Initial testing was structured around the platform's stated capabilities: for the Email AI module, this covered flagged-email handling, date filtering, thread-context retention, document retrieval, sent-item visibility, attachment handling, intelligent routing, a stated 25MB knowledge-base limit, and in-dashboard email management actions. For the attendance module, this covered average-working-hours calculation and attendance-abnormality detection.

05 — Root Cause / Findings
"Accepted" didn't always mean fixed

The vendor classified each submitted item as Accepted, Partially Accepted, or Not Planned. On follow-up verification, several items marked "Accepted" were still failing in practice — document retrieval returned a "no original file stored" error, attachments weren't visible in the interface at all, intelligent routing wasn't present in the UI despite being marked resolved, and a knowledge-base upload limit stated as 200MB in the dashboard actually failed on an 85MB file.

That same verification pass also surfaced defects outside the original test scope — reply-all silently dropping recipients, the AI agent showing no persistent memory of prior corrections, and the compose interface losing cursor focus after every message sent.

07 — Verification Rounds
What closed, what stayed open

A fix round delivered by the vendor was independently re-verified in the following cycle. Resolved in that pass: flagged-email indicators, date filtering, knowledge-base upload and retrieval, intelligent routing, reply-all recipient handling, attachment visibility, and the compose-focus issue. Still open at the close of this cycle: attachment content summarization had no assigned milestone, and two further items — external-mail segregation and persistent agent memory — were explicitly deferred by the vendor to a later delivery milestone rather than resolved.

08 — Security Considerations
Contract-level risk, reviewed before rollout

Before the platform was accepted, the professional services agreement itself was reviewed for risk — specifically intellectual-property ownership language and an incomplete deliverables schedule — and flagged as concerns ahead of commitment, treating vendor risk as more than a purely technical question.

09 — Validation
Nothing marked closed without independent re-testing

Every item the vendor reported as resolved was checked directly against the original requirement before being accepted as closed. This is what caught the gap between the vendor's "Accepted" classification and what was actually delivered in the first verification round.

10 — Result
From unverified claims to a tracked, largely-resolved state

The platform moved from a set of unverified vendor claims to a tracked, itemized state where the large majority of raised issues were independently confirmed as resolved, and the remaining gaps were explicitly identified and deferred to a defined milestone rather than silently dropped or left ambiguous.
